# Revision notes, version 2.0

## Material scholarly changes

- Reframed the manuscript as a conceptual, theory-building working paper rather than a product thesis or quasi-empirical report.
- Added a method and scope section that accurately describes a targeted integrative review, not a systematic review.
- Added a claim-status convention separating established evidence, author-derived arithmetic, conceptual propositions, synthetic illustrations, and design requirements.
- Defined judgment latency as a narrower component of broader decision latency and acknowledged the 2026 decision-latency literature.
- Removed the inaccessible Bodnar newsletter citation and its dependent “minutes versus months” section.
- Corrected the Microsoft 2025 interruption statistic to state that it was calculated for the top 20% of users by ping volume, with the report's exclusions.
- Removed the unsupported universal 20-minute recovery claim attributed to Mark et al. (2008).
- Replaced broad meeting claims with peer-reviewed meeting science and explicit limits on consulting evidence.
- Narrowed claims about digital records. The paper no longer says that almost every judgment is captured, that a typical role has hundreds of thousands of items, that the enterprise necessarily owns the corpus, or that most records are never read again.
- Converted architecture statements into falsifiable design requirements and introduced consequence-based authority levels.
- Added historical bias, incomplete records, privilege, concept drift, contestability, deskilling, false authority, and rebound effects as central risks.
- Removed the Talent Lab anecdote from the academic argument to avoid conflating a client outcome with WisdomTwin traction or product validation.
- Added six testable propositions and a staged evaluation design beginning with retrospective testing and prospective shadow mode.
- Replaced the overlapping meeting-message-interruption equation with mutually exclusive time buckets and explicit validation burden.
- Added a source-verification log, complete disclosures, version history, repository metadata, and a venue-neutral cover note.
- Removed uncited or nonessential book entries so every final bibliography entry supports a retained in-text claim and provides a DOI or authoritative URL.
- Preserved WT-100-001 as the stable series record while separating it from the document version number.
- Added Roman Bodnarchuk's verified ORCID iD, 0009-0004-3113-2118, to the manuscript and repository metadata.
- Assigned the CC BY 4.0 license for the public preprint release and added the license to the manuscript and repository metadata.

## McKinsey correction

The manuscript now reproduces the assumptions stated by De Smet, Jost, and Weiss (2019):

`56,400 × 0.20 × 220 × 0.37 × 0.58 = 532,551 manager-days`

This reasonably rounds to the reported “about 530,000” manager-days. The USD 250 million wage figure remains attributed to McKinsey. The weekly restatement is shown separately:

`5 × 0.37 × 0.58 = 1.073 workdays per five-day week`

The paper labels this as author-derived arithmetic from McKinsey's assumptions. It is not described as a McKinsey-reported weekly finding, a universal manager average, a regulated-industry estimate, or a WisdomTwin savings estimate.
